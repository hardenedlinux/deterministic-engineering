This specification continues 10.39 from an identified, completed ASCENT query
view into project review, authenticated disposition and recipient use. It designs
the complete target architecture, including direct refactoring of current case
adapters. Native POO Contracts, Core verification, Aitia review/history/authority
and existing source custody remain the owners. There is no second review engine,
fact store, role directory, receipt registry or version-management service.

The Library remains a reusable engineering Library delivered to Aitia projects.
DE-CASE-01 is this repository's own design-review case, not institutional
certification or an automatically authorized Library release. Its architecture,
Org products and decisions are review inputs with explicit verification duties.
The requested declaration generation remains V1, read from the native Contract
owner designed in 10.37. This document assigns no new generation, adds no version
to a namespace and does not treat a project revision as a contract version.

The policy's semantic revision identifies its rules; the provider source revision
identifies their implementation. Project revision identifies the reviewed target
occurrence, including relevant dirty/untracked inputs and dependencies. Review
record identity and evidence attempt identity identify events. These are separate
associations, inherited/read through their native owners, with no lexical or
numeric ordering assumed between opaque revision strings.

The reusable policy composes with a model/slice; it neither inherits the model's
project authority nor establishes a NASA standard adoption. A new general design
review policy must publish its actual requirements rather than translate DE-O
identifiers into fabricated NASA SWE requirements. Existing standard providers
apply only when the project has genuinely adopted their standard and mappings.
Shared context/content verification mechanics belong in native Aitia; NASA-specific
classification and clauses remain in the NASA provider. If the current native
request/type shape cannot express the selected policy, refactor that owner and
qualify its callers directly; do not bypass it with an approved? packet field.

| Role | Accountable act / boundary |
|------+----------------------------|
| Project owner / adoption authority | Establish subject/scope, policy adoption and actual role mandates; authorize exceptions only within that mandate |
| Content steward / architecture owner | Own Org products, model/source references, decisions, assumptions and revision completeness; correct input gaps |
| Trusted technical verifier | Check native C/Q/W/use and actual content/context under the selected operation; issue scoped Core verification through its actual adapter |
| Semantic reviewer | Evaluate architecture rationale, model applicability, assumptions, alternatives and counter evidence; record findings rather than infer them from a digest |
| Decision authority | Authenticate the scoped accept/reject/reconsideration decision and explicit treatment of previous denials; cannot manufacture missing technical evidence |
| Delivery owner | Bind authorized distribution to exact reviewed content, recipient and access; record transfer and limitations |
| Recipient owner | Verify provenance/access/current status, adopt the Library in its own project and admit the intended use; deployment requires its separate authority |

A policy may allow a person to hold multiple roles or require independence for
particular acts. The selected rule must be checked against actual authenticated
principals, including delegation and conflicts of interest. Agent authorship does
not appoint the Agent to a reviewer or decision role. DE-CASE-01's unfilled roles
from C1 remain unfilled until actual assignment; this design supplies no signatures.

* SPECIFIED Review input and deterministic query consumption
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: exact-query-review-request
:END:

C, A, E, W and Q retain the meanings in 10.38/10.39. R is a prepared review
request over that input/evidence/query basis. A disposition record and a delivery
manifest are native values/references owned by the relevant operation; these names
do not prescribe another wire schema or a central object manager.

R binds the following together before verification:

| Binding | Required input / check |
|---------+------------------------|
| Selected native association | C and actual Library role descriptors, shared native Contract owner, real parent chain, model/slice/policy identities and semantic revisions; validator selected from the trusted required boundary |
| Project and purpose | Actual subject/revision/repository, reviewed scope, design-review purpose, adoption, Profile selection and captured project inputs; provider checkout remains distinct from target repo |
| Exact query | Q identity and accepted program/catalog/engine/source-set, completed finite query and filter, rule closure, C/A/W/use associations, outcomes, explanations and coverage limits from 10.39 |
| Evidence and products | Required DE-O obligations and expected document/model/decision inventory independently of available witnesses; exact D01-D10 product sections/bytes, model mappings, attempts, artifacts, methods and qualified uses |
| Review context | Actual responsibility assignments/grants, policy applicability, outstanding assumptions, risk/exception rules, trusted history/decision source inventory and current source cut |
| Intended downstream use | Recipient and purpose when delivery is requested, allowed scope and accessibility requirements; an unspecified recipient cannot receive a scope-specific use approval |

The Scenario adapter consumes Q as an identified native result, not a reconstructed
list of favorable rows. It verifies ownership/accepted-source association and
required scope against C and the actual query owner. Serialized rows, a view
hash, a screenshot or a public checked flag are inspectable artifacts, not native
issuance. Imported results require a separately qualified provenance/verification
route. An unsupported route remains a gap.

The selected policy declares the queries and coverage needed for review. User
filters may aid inspection but cannot reduce the required scope, hide counters
or remove pending duties. Each relevant row/explanation is traced to its actual
application, attempt, witness and checked use. A completed empty result does not
prove all duties discharged; explicit expected obligations/product inventory and
query completeness determine that. Counter, pending and conflict remain visible
when mapped into Aitia: SdlcEvidence's current pass/fail shape cannot encode them
by calling unknown pass or dropping conflicts. Refactor the native assessment
input/result representation where needed and qualify the selected policy's gate.

ASCENT computes deterministic consequences for explicit finite accepted inputs;
Aitia authenticates context/content and applies the selected review policy. It
does not silently rerun a different query or treat inferred support as a human
approval. New evidence, source set, rules, policy or request scope creates the
appropriate new C/application/use/Q/R associations. Q remains immutable history
when its current applicability expires. A decision history update alone need not
rerun Rust/QNT/Lean/SysML; it requires fresh current discovery/review assessment.
If the selected query actually includes that history, its changed input also
requires a newly identified Q. Captured adoption, role assignment or policy-input
changes require a fresh appropriate capture; a later status check does not silently
replace those captured inputs. Independent withdrawal/status checks can invalidate
current eligibility without rewriting the original C.

* SPECIFIED Context, content and decision verification
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: context-content-and-disposition
:END:

| Operation / input | Output and retained artifact | Responsible trust boundary |
|-------------------+------------------------------+----------------------------|
| Prepare R from C/Q and required review scope | Readable dossier linking exact products, evidence, gaps, history and role bindings; structured native request association | Scenario preparation only; not a verdict or authority receipt |
| Verify project context | Scoped context request and real Core verification receipt, or explicit unresolved/refused finding | Trusted verifier checks actual repo/revision/scope, policy adoption/applicability, project classification when relevant, roles/grants and expected source inventory |
| Verify content | Scoped content requests and receipts plus product/trace/semantic findings | Read exact referenced bytes/sections/models and claims; verify Q association, witnesses, input footprints, assumptions, decisions and reference closure. File existence/hash checks alone establish integrity, not content adequacy |
| Discover history and assess technical obligations | Exact required-source discovery and current technical assessment, including unresolved failures/conflicts | Existing history/source owners and the selected native policy; no complete? asserted by a packet author |
| Review semantics and resolve findings | Attributed findings, requested changes, accepted limitations or properly authorized exceptions | Actual appointed reviewer; independent technical controls where required. Unsupported assumptions remain explicit obligations |
| Decide under policy | Authenticated scoped accept/reject decision and rationale bound to R, verification basis, history cut and prior records | Actual decision authority with verified grants/identity and required coverage; proof of decision signing is distinct from truth of the reviewed claims |
| Evaluate current review admission | Existing native authority gate plus qualified selected-policy checks, or blocked/rejected/incomplete result | Real current receipts, discovery and actual authority inputs; the public assessment is not a transferable authority token |

Context verification and content verification use distinct purposes/snapshots
but share the same R binding. Context success cannot stand in for content
success, reviewer approval cannot stand in for either, and semantic support
cannot stand in for actual appointment. The trusted adapter checks the selected
policy and operation, exact request, actual inputs and current dependencies both
at issuance and subsequent use. It owns acceptable verification duration and
clock assumptions; a caller cannot choose an unlimited approval lifetime.

Core's existing verification machinery remains the sole local receipt issuer:
its private adapter association, snapshot, interval and revocation are checked
through the native APIs. The verifier operation must itself establish the facts;
snapshot stability is not an atomic global filesystem capture or semantic proof.
Copying a receipt, mutating the request, replaying a receipt at another revision,
using an unissued adapter or revoking a receipt must prevent current admission.
A serialized packet is retained for explanation and transport, not deserialized
into the original process's issuance table.

A decision records actual signer/role/grant, R and reviewed query/evidence basis,
policy/project revision/scope, findings/rationale, unresolved duties, permitted
exceptions, prior/superseded decision references and validity/reconsideration
conditions. The decision must identify what it accepts: for example bounded
design adequacy rather than native runtime safety or a recipient deployment.
An accepted exception records authorized risk owner, scope, rationale, mitigation,
expiry and review trigger. It does not change a failed property into support.

The current native authority gate requires verified discovery, technical admission,
current-policy approval and no unresolved verified denial; it does not by itself
enforce arbitrary project quorum, independence, exception semantics or delivery.
The actual trusted approval operation and qualified native policy adapter must
check these rules before their receipts are usable. Extend that native owner
when necessary; do not infer these protections from its existing admitted? field.
The existing gate's release-authorized? false is preserved as its authority limit.

* SPECIFIED Counter evidence, conflict, reassessment and revocation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: review-history-and-revocation
:END:

| Event | Required response and retained evidence | Consequence for current use |
|-------+-----------------------------------------+-----------------------------|
| Property counter, including a successful M06 negative control | Keep actual claim/polarity/C/A/E/W and applicable query explanation; distinguish test-harness expectation from property truth | No positive safety disposition for that same claim. Narrowing a use needs explicit applicability justification; excluded counters remain visible in the relevant history |
| Support and counter for one applicable duty/basis | Retain both, determine differing assumptions/runs/footprints and assess conflict under the policy | Required conflict blocks admission until explained/resolved; newest or favorable result does not automatically win |
| Missing/failed/timeout method or missing required product | Retain attempt/diagnostics, required duty and gap; unavailable artifacts do not become absent obligations | Pending/incomplete for the affected purpose; no routine pass/fail conversion merely from command exit |
| Duplicate identity with incompatible content, divergent decision branches or contradictory role grants | Reject ambiguous binding and retain offending records; consult the actual authoritative source inventory | Refuse current decision/use until a qualified authority resolves the scope; no lexical revision or last-file-wins rule |
| Source, mapping, model, tool, rule, project or policy change | Apply 10.39 footprints and 10.38 input capture; establish successor project occurrence and new affected Q/R; record impact/rationale | Reuse only through actual fresh applicability verification; old approval does not follow a revision string change |
| Repair of a failed case | Link old failure to exact changed inputs, new attempt/result, correspondence and scoped repair justification; independently verify the repair/disposition | Original counter remains history. Repair may discharge its current duty only under the selected policy; a changed digest alone is insufficient |
| Previous rejection or policy replacement | Discover complete relevant accepted decision history, including negative decisions under previous policy; name the exact record and authority for any supersession | Current native gate retains negative decisions across policy changes. A verified current-policy approval may supersede named denials only when the trusted operation also validates that authority and scope |
| Reviewer grant revoked, receipt revoked/expired, source unavailable or new accepted denial | Preserve historical issuance and decisions; refresh authority/discovery and current applicability | Refuse affected current admission; prior verification success is not a durable permission |
| Review owner retirement, restart, recovery or handover | Retain accepted prefix and source references; verify actual root/head/prefix/reader and authorized handover through existing custody owners | Recovery must establish current owner authority and fresh native verification; archive consistency or copied receipt bytes cannot restore it |

Reassessment is an append-only association of predecessor review, changed basis,
impact allocation, new verification, retained findings and authenticated decision.
It does not mutate the old project capture, witness or review. Project owners
establish actual revision lineage; no semantic ordering is inferred from r1/r2 or
opaque labels. Supersession edges must be unambiguous, authorized, scope-correct
and acyclic. A repair finding, a denial supersession, a grant revocation and a
Core receipt revocation are different owner operations, not one status toggle.

Discovery must close over the authoritative required technical and decision
sources, predecessor/handover sources and accepted decisions, with each source
available and current. Required inventory comes from actual trusted project
mandate/custody, not from only the sources a caller happens to supply. Missing
sources, disappeared intakes, forked heads or an unverified negative record
cannot be omitted to make approval possible. Malformed unauthenticated submissions
remain diagnostic attempts; they are not accepted authority denials.

Aitia's authority assessment consumes verified current source discovery; any
ASCENT projection of disposition/history is derived from accepted native facts.
ASCENT cannot create reviewer grants or erase retained decisions. If a new review
creates new history facts, use a subsequent identified query when needed; never
insert the decision back into its own input hash or retroactively change Q/R.

Revocation has two layers: Core removes local receipt validity; actual project
owners record and discover authority withdrawal or review/use restrictions.
Recipients recheck the relevant current status before the governed operation.
There is no claim of instantaneous global revocation across disconnected hosts.
Offline use is permitted only if an actual adopted policy explicitly bounds its
scope/freshness and accepts that delay; otherwise current status unavailable
means admission unavailable. Revocation does not erase a historical transfer or
undo an executed deployment; downstream response needs its own authorized act.

* SPECIFIED Delivery and recipient admission
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: recipient-delivery-and-use
:END:

Review disposition, permission to distribute, transfer receipt, recipient
verification, project adoption, use admission and deployment are distinct acts.
A review result is handed to the actual receiving owner together with its basis
and restrictions. Neither a rendered D2 nor a producer's review-admitted label
executes any of these downstream acts.

| Handoff step | Inputs | Output / document artifact and acceptance |
|--------------+--------+-------------------------------------------|
| Qualify distribution | Exact reviewed R/disposition and current policy/project/role/history bindings; actual recipient/purpose and distribution mandate | Authorized distribution scope, or blocked reason. Recipient and allowed artifacts/access must be concrete |
| Assemble delivery | Exact Library native publication and selected interface/role Contracts; reviewed Org/model/decision products; C/Q/evidence and decision provenance | Delivery manifest locating exact content identities/bytes, expected inventory, dependencies/tools, source/artifact access, limits, retained counters/gaps and current-status discovery. Contents are referenced before the manifest is identified; no self-hash cycle |
| Export trust material | Real issuer/decision identity, authority grants, signed records where supported, verification method and trust roots, validity/status sources | Inspectable provenance. Core process-local receipts are explanatory references; any exportable attestation route must itself be qualified and its scope checked |
| Transfer and acknowledge | Authorized manifest/content and actual receiver/channel/access | Authenticated acknowledgement of exactly what arrived, missing references and available access. Transport success or checksum match does not imply review acceptance |
| Verify at recipient | Received bytes/provenance, required dependencies/artifacts, actual trusted roots/status sources and recipient verifier | Fresh recipient-owned verification of integrity, content and current authority at the required strength, with explicit gaps; local producer private issuance is not imported as live authority |
| Adopt and bind project use | Recipient's actual Profile, project revision/repo, native Contract/role compatibility, policy adoption and role mandate | Recipient Scenario capture and scoped use/review association; reuse of old evidence requires 10.39's fresh qualified applicability, or rerun affected methods |
| Admit intended operation | Recipient-bound context/content/history and actual decision/operation authority | Recipient-owned use decision plus receipt/record of its scope. Separate explicit authority is still needed for release/deployment |

A delivery contract is the native operation's expected Contract over these
associations, not a duplicate JSON governance system. Shared in-process delivery
may retain actual handles when their owner/custody remains available. Cross-process
or cross-host delivery requires real recipient-side verification and any actual
exportable signed provenance. Existing local signature/archive code demonstrates
bounded custody mechanisms; a general cross-host Library handoff and revocation
channel are not already qualified. If no qualified issuer route exists, deliver
inspectable source/artifacts and re-establish the necessary recipient evidence;
do not claim to reproduce an unobserved original execution.

The Library publishes reusable model/policy/method Contracts. Its DE-CASE-01
approval covers the reviewed provider occurrence and purpose. A network-service
repo selects suitable model/slice/policy, captures its own architecture, source,
parameters, documents and roles, and establishes its own Q/R. It may reuse exact
qualified evidence only for matching claims/footprints and permitted scope.
Provider approval does not approve that service's runtime, architecture or deployment.
Reading V1 from the same native generation owner is required; it does not prove
compatibility of changed semantic revisions, providers or execution premises.

D01-D10 may remain sections of a bounded Org dossier. The manifest resolves their
exact section/content and transitive references; it does not require ten files.
A local .data path without accessible retained bytes is not a portable delivery.
Missing proprietary or external evidence must have an authorized inspectable
access route or remain an explicit gap. A redacted/subset package is a newly
identified view whose claimed review scope and verification basis must be checked;
it cannot inherit an unqualified full-package approval. Relative artifact relocation
may preserve bytes, but changed custody/resolution/access requires verification.

An acknowledgement, verification report and final use decision identify the
recipient, manifest/content, operation and actual authority. Persist their records
through existing custody owners; do not add an independent shadow decision log.
Later revocation/reassessment uses those recipient associations for authorized
notification and re-evaluation. Merely recording an intended recipient does not
notify or grant them authority.

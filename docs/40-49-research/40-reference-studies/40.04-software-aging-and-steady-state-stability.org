* Research Problem Statement
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: aging-problem-statement
:STATUS: mechanism-first-study
:END:

Long-running software systems routinely pass unit tests, integration suites, and
short-duration benchmarks, yet progressively degrade over hours or days of
continuous execution: resident memory climbs without plateau, tail latency
widens, garbage collection or allocator overhead accelerates, and execution
units (OS threads, goroutines, async tasks, open descriptors) accumulate until
stall or OOM termination.

This study grounds long-running stability in *language-agnostic software and
systems engineering mechanisms* rather than treating it as a Rust-only or
Go-only tuning problem. The dependency direction follows the repository's core
ownership boundary ([[file:../../10-19-design/10-architecture/10.14-rust-performance-library-and-mechanism-cases.org][10.14]]):

#+begin_example
Empirical & Systems Mechanisms
  -> Language-Agnostic Formal Models (Quint finite countermodels + Lean unbounded laws)
  -> Language Projections (C, C++, Rust, Go, TypeScript/Node, JVM)
  -> Independent Mechanical Oracles (CBMC, Static AST Auditors, Compressed-Budget Slope Oracles)
  -> Closed-Loop AI Agent Evidence & Governance
#+end_example

The motivating research question for AI-assisted software engineering is sharp:
*Can AI-authored code achieve long-running, steady-state, high-performance
stability comparable to—or systematically more dependable than—experienced
systems engineers?* Empirical studies show that LLMs optimize locally for
happy-path functional pass rates while systematically under-specifying
cross-time resource conservation, error/cancellation cleanup paths, watermark
bounding, and overload feedback stability. Closing this gap requires encoding
decades of empirical systems-reliability knowledge into *deterministic,
mechanically verifiable contracts* rather than relying on prompt intuition.

* Six Language-Agnostic Engineering Mechanisms and Canonical Evidence

|----+-----------------------+-----------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+------------------------------|
| =SM1= | *Software Aging & Aging-Related Bugs (ARBs)* | Monotonic state/error accumulation with uptime or cycle count; failure probability grows over time even without new code faults | Huang et al. | =SM2= | *Resource Conservation & Error-Path Closure* | Handles, permits, sockets, locks, or memory leaked on rare error, timeout, panic, or early-return paths | Ding Yuan et al. | =SM3= | *Memory Bloat, Watermark Ratcheting & Fragmentation* | Memory climbs on traffic spikes and never returns; or live heap stays flat while RSS / GC pause time creeps upward | Jump & McKinley (POPL 2007 — Cork semantic leaks); Guoqing Xu et al. (PLDI/ISSTA — Memory Bloat / LeakChaser); Cotroneo et al. (SRDS 2007 — JVM aging); Powers, Tench, Berger, McGregor (PLDI 2019 — Mesh compacting allocator breaking fragmentation bounds in Redis/Firefox) | Cap reusable pool retention (=max_retain_cap= + =pool_slot_cap=); prove quiescent retained bytes independent of peak burst size =S=; monitor RSS vs. live-bytes divergence |
| =SM4= | *Structured Concurrency & Cancellation Quiescence* | Orphaned threads, goroutines, or async tasks pile up when callers time out or cancel; blocked channels/locks never drain | Joe Armstrong (2003 PhD Thesis — Erlang/OTP supervision & isolation); Martin Sustrik & N. (ASPLOS 2019 — Go concurrency bugs in K8s/etcd/Docker/CockroachDB); Boqin Qin et al. | =SM5= | *End-to-End Backpressure, Overload Control & Metastable Immunity* | A transient trigger (GC pause, downstream hiccup, burst) pushes the service into a self-sustaining degraded state that fails to recover after load drops | Matt Welsh et al. (SOSP 2001 — SEDA); Dean & Barroso (CACM 2013 — Tail at Scale); Zhou et al. (SoCC 2018 — DAGOR WeChat queue-delay load shedding); Cho et al. (OSDI 2020 — Breakwater credit admission); Bronson et al. | =SM6= | *Honest Open-Loop Measurement, DST & Time-Compressed Soak* | Closed-loop tests hide stalls; short runs miss slow leaks; production discovers degradation hours after release | Gil Tene (Coordinated Omission & HDRHistogram); Zhou et al. Every mechanism above manifests through distinct language-runtime idioms.
Fixing one language without capturing the underlying mechanism leaves the same
structural flaw open in every sibling stack.

* Why AI-Generated Code Fails Long-Running Stability — and Deterministic Remedies
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ai-aging-remedies
:END:

Empirical evaluations of LLM code generation show a sharp divergence between
short-lived functional correctness (e.g., passing unit tests) and production
long-running reliability:

1. *Local Optimization vs. Global Conservation*: LLMs generate functions token-by-token
   to satisfy immediate test assertions, omitting cross-boundary ownership transfer,
   cleanup on rare error/cancel interleavings, and pool-size ceilings.
2. *Happy-Path & Tutorial Corpus Bias*: Training corpora frequently omit =defer=,
   =Close()=, =ctx.Done()= channel select branches, =max_retain_cap= checks, and
   retry budgets for brevity.
3. *Runtime Surface Illusions*: Models routinely assume =drop(join_handle)= cancels
   an async task, =vec.clear()= or =delete(m, k)= frees OS memory, bounded queues
   alone bound resident memory, or defensive retries automatically increase reliability
   (ignoring metastable amplification).
4. *Missing Temporal Derivative Checks*: Standard agent verification checks point
   outputs (=f(x) == y=), never steady-state conservation across fault-injected
   cycles ($\Delta_{\text{handles}} = 0$, $\text{slope}(\text{retained}) \approx 0$).

To lift AI-authored engineering to deterministic long-running stability, this
repository pairs five mechanical layers:

1. *Formal Mechanism Contracts (Quint + Lean)*:
   - Finite-state protocol models with emergent invariants and mandatory
     guard-pruned countermodels (Apalache =0.62.1= + 100% =--mbt= action coverage):
     - =engineering.concurrency.quiescence-resource-drain= ([[file:../../../packages/quint-models/engineering/concurrency/quiescence-resource-drain/README.org][QuiescenceResourceDrain.qnt]]) — =SM2= / =SM4=
     - =engineering.concurrency.buffer-ratchet-reclamation= ([[file:../../../packages/quint-models/engineering/concurrency/buffer-ratchet-reclamation/README.org][BufferRatchetReclamation.qnt]]) — =SM3=
     - =engineering.concurrency.metastable-retry-collapse= ([[file:../../../packages/quint-models/engineering/concurrency/metastable-retry-collapse/README.org][MetastableRetryCollapse.qnt]]) — =SM5=
     - =engineering.concurrency.slow-consumer-eviction= ([[file:../../../packages/quint-models/engineering/concurrency/slow-consumer-eviction/README.org][SlowConsumerEviction.qnt]]) — =SM4b= / =SM5b=
     - =engineering.gc.gc-pacing-thrash-guard= ([[file:../../../packages/quint-models/engineering/gc/gc-pacing-thrash-guard/README.org][GcPacingThrashGuard.qnt]]) — =SM3b=
     - =engineering.concurrency.bounded-projection-eviction= ([[file:../../../packages/quint-models/engineering/concurrency/bounded-projection-eviction/README.org][BoundedProjectionEviction.qnt]]) — =SM3c= (Marlin =BridgeState= / session LRU / cascade side-map cleanup)
     - =engineering.concurrency.stream-lag-resync-coalescing= ([[file:../../../packages/quint-models/engineering/concurrency/stream-lag-resync-coalescing/README.org][StreamLagResyncCoalescing.qnt]]) — =SM5c= (broadcast lag snapshot-resync & MyGo coalesced checkpoint flush)
     - =engineering.concurrency.ui-render-window-budget= ([[file:../../../packages/quint-models/engineering/concurrency/ui-render-window-budget/README.org][UiRenderWindowBudget.qnt]]) — =SM7= (WebView bounded DOM window & $O(1)$ incremental rendering)
     - Paired with the 3 UI concurrency kernel models (=ui-mainthread-rcu-snapshot=, =fenced-coalescing-mailbox=, =hybrid-webview-focus-lifecycle=) refined directly in Go via [[file:../../../languages-engineering/go/concurrency/stability_soak_test.go][concurrency/stability_soak_test.go]].
   - Unbounded algebraic proofs in Lean 4 ([[file:../../../packages/lean-models/DeterministicEngineering/Core/Stability.lean][Core/Stability.lean]]):
     - Arbitrary-cycle zero-drift conservation + proof that *any* positive per-fault
       leak rate $\ge 1$ exhausts *any* finite capacity $C$ in $\le C$ fault cycles;
     - Spike-independent quiescent memory ceiling under capped buffer return;
     - Bounded total load under explicit retry budget vs. unbounded amplification;
     - GC soft-memory-limit headroom floor & pacing cycle bound;
     - Slow-consumer fan-out total byte bound vs. single-stalled-consumer budget exhaustion;
     - Bounded projection idle-eviction liveness (preventing bounded-without-eviction lockout);
     - Lag snapshot-resync alignment & coalesced write-amplification reduction vs. quadratic per-token rewrites;
     - UI viewport virtualization cap & $O(1)$ incremental render independence vs. full-transcript re-parse budget breach.
2. *Multi-Language Paired Contrasts, Go UI Kernel & Real Runtime Telemetry Soak*:
   - Side-by-side =bad_aging= vs. =deterministic_steady= projections across
     C (with CBMC formal check), C++, Rust (with slow-consumer eviction, Marlin
     bridge eviction/lag-resync soak & OLS slope), TypeScript (including

     and Go (via modular =devenv=, covering both =stability/aging-contrast/= and
     the hardened =concurrency/= UI kernel with =testing/synctest=, live Quint
     ITF replay, real =runtime.ReadMemStats=, =debug.SetMemoryLimit= =GOMEMLIMIT=,
     and =net.Pipe()= slow-consumer tests).
3. *MBT State-Machine Refinement*:
   - Live Quint =--mbt= ITF traces replayed against stateful TypeScript SUTs
     across all 8 formal stability models plus Go ITF replay across all 3 UI
     concurrency models (11 formal models refined end-to-end).
4. *Time-Compressed Steady-State Slope Oracle (=SM6=)*:
   - Independent harness running warmup + open-loop chaotic fault/spike/cancel
     pulse cycles under tightened capacity ceilings, asserting:
     - exact zero discrete drift ($\Delta = 0$ on handles, timers, active tasks, permits, orphaned side maps);
     - bounded burst peak and guaranteed quiescent return below baseline ceiling;
     - non-positive linear regression slope over multi-cycle quiescent snapshots.
5. *Multi-Language Static Aging-Trap Auditor & Closed-Loop AI Repair Governance*:
   - Structural AST/pattern auditor + =agent-stability-audit.ts= CLI

     and live downstream audit calibration against =marlin-agent-core= + MyGo; see

* Qualification Scope and Explicit Limits
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: aging-limits
:END:

- Finite Quint models establish bounded protocol safety and guard necessity
  counterexamples within their declared step bounds; they do not model physical
  OS virtual-memory allocators, kernel TCP state machines, or real-time hardware
  clocks.
- Lean 4 proofs establish unbounded algebraic conservation and exhaustion laws
  over abstract natural-number models; applying them to a concrete binary still
  requires source-to-model correspondence and native runtime evidence.
- Compressed-budget Slope Oracles accelerate logical leaks, watermark ratchets,
  and metastable retry traps from hours into milliseconds, but physical allocator
  fragmentation (e.g., =glibc= vs. =jemalloc= page-decay curves under production
  allocation distributions) additionally requires target-platform RSS/pprof
  soak measurements.
- Where a language toolchain is absent on a verification host (for example, if
  =go= is not installed locally), source-authored language projections remain
  explicitly distinguished from locally executed verification receipts.
